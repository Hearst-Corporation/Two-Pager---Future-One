What drives the curve 03

Most capex (electrical + cooling MEP, ~75-85%) scales linearly per MW — it does not get cheaper. The economy of scale comes entirely from the fixed site bucket (~10-25% of capex) being spread over more megawatts.

Substation & grid connection
A 100 MW substation does not cost 2× a 50 MW one. Large transformers price at $15-30/kVA vs $40-70 for small. Per-MW grid cost drops ~20-50% at scale.
Soft costs & design
Engineering, permits, PM (8-15% of capex) are largely fixed per site. Spread over 300 MW vs 30 MW, the $/MW falls ~10×; reused across a fleet, further still.
Land & site spine
One parcel, one perimeter, one access road, one NOC — bought once for the full build-out. Land at ~0.5 acre/MW dilutes; in Qatar free-zone allocation makes it ~1-3% of capex.
Bulk procurement
Gensets, switchgear, UPS, chillers bought direct/OEM at volume. The lever behind the hyperscaler $6M/MW target — and the floor that linear MEP imposes.

The scaling rule. Cost/MW follows $/MW = 7.8 + K·MW^(−0.61) — a hyperbola with a hard floor. Doubling capacity cuts $/MW by ~12% early, fading to <1% past 200 MW. You capture ~80% of the total scale benefit by 150 MW; beyond that the linear MEP/cooling cost dominates and the curve is essentially flat.
